# NHPL Evidence Methodology — Draft

**Status**: Work in progress (decisions captured through 2026-03-17 session)
**Purpose**: Comprehensive methodology for how NHPL finds, appraises, contextualises, and publishes health evidence for Nepal

---

## 1. Scope & Objectives

**What NHPL's evidence portal does**: Systematically curates, appraises, and contextualises global health evidence for Nepal's policymakers, practitioners, and public.

**Philosophy**: AI-assisted, human-verified. AI does the heavy lifting (extraction, structuring, translation drafts). Humans do the thinking (appraisal, contextualisation, final sign-off).

**Framing**: Cast wide, tag precisely. Broad on inclusion, rigorous on labelling. Users filter by rigour level — we don't exclude for them.

## 6. Intake Channels

### Channel 1: Systematic Discovery (automated)
- Core databases: PubMed, EconLit, Google Scholar, Scopus, 3ie
- Monthly scan ritual (one morning per month, saved searches)
- PubMed and Google Scholar alerts for priority topics
- Documented with search strings, reproducible

### Channel 2: Expert Recommendation (targeted)
- Advisors, PhD supervisors, network contacts nominate papers directly
- Reviewers flag important papers from their own research
- No paper is turned away because it didn't come from a search — if someone with domain expertise says "this needs to be here," it enters the pipeline
- Source channel recorded (who recommended and why)

### Additional intake
- Pragmatic/snowball: reference list scanning, citation tracking
- Review journal scoping (JEL, Cochrane, Lancet commissions)

All channels feed into the same verification pipeline. No difference in review rigour.

### Search Management Tools
- **Zotero**: Reference management, shared group library for team
- **Rayyan**: Screening, deduplication, dual screening with conflict detection (free)

## 7. Source Hierarchy & Appraisal Tiers

### Tier 1: High-prestige journals — Light appraisal, heavy contextualisation

**Journals**: Top 5 economics, top field journals (JHE, Health Economics, JDE, AEJ:Applied), Lancet, BMJ, NEJM, Lancet Global Health, BMJ Global Health, Health Policy and Planning, Social Science & Medicine, Cochrane Reviews

- Already through rigorous peer review
- Appraisal focus: contextualisation for Nepal, transferability assessment, policy implications
- Still document: study design, sample size, setting, key limitations
- Do not re-do the referee's job on identification strategy / internal validity

### Tier 2: Good journals — Moderate appraisal + contextualisation

**Tier 2a (selective journals)**: IJHPM, Bulletin of WHO, HPP (if not already Tier 1), well-established development/health journals

**Tier 2b (broad-scope journals)**: PLOS One, BMC series, Frontiers, well-known working paper series (NBER by established authors, World Bank Policy Research)

- Standard critical appraisal: study design appropriateness, sample size and power, key confounders, generalisability
- For econometric papers: note identification strategy, discuss compliers if IV, check parallel trends if DiD, consider external validity
- Full contextualisation for Nepal
- Working papers assessed by author reputation and methodology, not just the series

### Tier 3: Nepal-specific, grey literature, and pre-prints — Deep appraisal + contextualisation

**Sources**: NHRC journal, JNMA, Nepal-specific studies in smaller journals, MoHP/DoHS reports, donor evaluations, working papers from lesser-known institutions, ALL pre-prints (medRxiv, bioRxiv, SSRN drafts)

- Full methodological scrutiny
- For quantitative: sample size adequacy, sampling strategy, measurement validity, statistical methods, confounding, bias risk
- For econometric: identification strategy credibility, complier population, instrument validity, effect size plausibility
- For qualitative: sampling, saturation, reflexivity, transferability
- For grey literature: AACODS framework (Authority, Accuracy, Coverage, Objectivity, Date, Significance)
- Full contextualisation for Nepal

### Deep Appraisal Specifics

- **Sample size**: Not just "n=500" — what does it mean for statistical power? Can this study detect policy-meaningful effect sizes?
- **IV studies**: Who are the compliers? Is the LATE relevant to Nepal's target population? Is the instrument plausible in Nepal's context?
- **DiD**: Are parallel trends credible? What happened differently in treatment vs control?
- **RCTs**: External validity — does trial setting resemble Nepal? Hawthorne effects? Implementation fidelity?
- **Qualitative**: Sample diversity, member-checking, cultural transferability to Nepal
- **Cost-effectiveness**: Whose costs? Transferable to Nepal's price levels and health system structure?

## 8. AI Pipeline & Human Verification Workflow

### Processing Flow

```
Paper enters (any channel)
    ↓
Tier classification (by source journal/series)
    ↓
Semantic Scholar API → auto-fills bibliographic info
(title, authors, journal, year, abstract, citations)
    ↓
Custom AI pipeline → extracts:
- Study design, sample size, setting
- Key findings, effect sizes
- What Was Studied, What They Found
    ↓
Nepal Contextualisation Layer → AI generates
"What This Means for Nepal" grounded in Nepal
RAG knowledge base (not LLM parametric knowledge)
    ↓
QA Check → AI reviews output against source,
flags low-confidence sections
    ↓
Output: draft JSON entry
    ↓
ALL TIERS → Human review queue (NOT published)
    ↓
┌──────────────────┬──────────────────┐
│  Tier 1/2         │  Tier 3           │
│                   │                   │
│  Single reviewer  │  Dual review      │
│  Full review      │  (methods +       │
│  (~15-20 min)     │  domain expert)   │
│       ↓           │  (~90-150 min)    │
│  → verified       │       ↓           │
│    or revision    │  → verified       │
│                   │    or rejected    │
└──────────────────┴──────────────────┘
```

**Nothing is published before human verification.** No paper appears on the portal until at least one human reviewer has verified it. This is non-negotiable.

- **Pre-prints**: Always Tier 3. Must be human-verified before publication. Labelled as "pre-print — not yet peer reviewed."

### AI Pipeline Architecture

- **Semantic Scholar API** (free): Automated bibliographic verification, citation graphs, retraction status
- **Custom extraction pipeline**: Built with Claude/GPT, extracts structured data from paper text
- **Nepal RAG Knowledge Base**: Curated document of Nepal health system facts (NHIP stats, physician density, federal structure, key policies, demographics). Contextualisation is grounded in this knowledge base, not LLM parametric memory. Updated as Nepal's health system evolves.
- **Automated status monitoring agent**: Runs weekly, checks every DOI against CrossRef and Retraction Watch. Auto-flags retractions, errata, and pre-print-to-publication upgrades.

### Status Definitions

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| `draft` | AI-processed. In human review queue. NOT visible on portal. |
| `under-review` | Assigned to human reviewer(s). In progress. |
| `verified` | Human reviewer has signed off (see verification checklist below). Published on portal. |
| `rejected` | Reviewed and excluded. |
| `withdrawn` | Source paper retracted or fundamental error found. Remains with prominent withdrawal banner. |

## 9. Human Review Model — PhD Student Reviewers

### Structure

- Volunteer PhD students from diverse health-related domains
- Each reviewer covers their area of expertise
- At least dual review for Tier 3 papers (one methods person + one domain person)
- Disagreements resolved by senior reviewer (Sabin or named advisor)
- Single reviewer sufficient for Tier 1/2 contextualisation checks

### Recruitment

- Start with own networks: Strathclyde, UK global health PhD programmes (LSHTM, Edinburgh, Leeds, Liverpool), Nepali universities (TU IOM, KUSMS, PAHS, BPKIHS)
- Simple Google Form + one-page explainer
- Build a roster larger than needed at any time (PhD students get busy around submissions, vivas, teaching)

### Training Programme (3 sessions, ~8 hours total)

**Pre-Training (self-paced, ~2-3 hours)**
- Read Reviewer Handbook
- Read CAFE-HE framework (if methods reviewer)
- Read topic-specific guide documents
- Review 3 worked contextualisation examples

**Session 1: Foundations (~1.5 hours, live)**
- What NHPL does and why it matters
- The evidence pipeline: discovery to portal
- Tier system and what each tier requires
- Rating scales: methodological rigour vs Nepal applicability
- Q&A

**Session 2: Hands-On Practice (~2 hours, live)**
- Complete Tier 1/2 review together
- Complete Tier 3 review together
- Common AI errors to watch for
- Nepal contextualisation: good vs bad examples
- Practice exercise: independently review a provided paper

**Session 3: Calibration & Assessment (~1.5 hours, live)**
- Compare practice review results across trainees
- Discuss disagreements and calibrate standards
- Final assessment: independently review a test paper
- Pass threshold: assessment must align with gold standard
- Certification issued upon passing

**After Training**
- First 3 months: Tier 1/2 reviews only
- Graduate to Tier 3 after demonstrating consistent quality
- Quarterly calibration exercises for all active reviewers

### What Reviewers Get

- Named credit on every paper they review ("Reviewed by" on the paper summary page)
- Annual acknowledgement on the site
- Reference/recommendation letter when needed
- Exposure to diverse literature across their field
- CV line: "Evidence Reviewer, Nepal Health Policy Lab"
- Certificate: Training completion certificate upon passing assessment
- Verifiable experience: Evidence appraisal and critical analysis skills for PhD/job applications

### Rules

- Conflict of interest: A reviewer cannot appraise a paper by their own supervisor or close collaborator
- Throughput buffer: More reviewers on roster than needed at any time to account for PhD student availability fluctuations
- Roster planning: Recruit for 50% attrition within 6 months. Maintain 3:1 roster-to-active ratio. Target: 60-80 people on roster to sustain 20-30 active reviewers.

## 10. Verification Checklist

"Verified" means a reviewer has confirmed ALL of:

1. ✅ Bibliographic information is correct (title, authors, year, journal, DOI)
2. ✅ Study design classification is accurate
3. ✅ Methodological rigour rating is appropriate
3b. ✅ Nepal applicability rating is appropriate
4. ✅ One-pager summary accurately represents the paper's findings (no misrepresentation)
5. ✅ Nepal contextualisation is plausible and fair
6. ✅ Material limitations are noted
7. ✅ Policy implications are defensible

For Tier 3 papers, additionally:

8. ✅ Methodology is sound (or limitations clearly flagged)
9. ✅ Sample size / power is adequate for claimed conclusions
10. ✅ Identification strategy is credible (for econometric papers)
11. ✅ Findings are not overstated relative to the evidence

## 11. Role of Systematic Reviews

Systematic reviews and meta-analyses serve three functions — they are NOT treated the same as primary studies in NHPL's own synthesis process:

### Function 1: Scoping Tool (before search)
- Check existing systematic reviews before searching for primary studies on a topic
- Map the landscape: what's been studied, what hasn't, overall evidence direction
- Use as curated reading lists (mine reference lists for primary studies)
- Review journals (JEL, Cochrane, Lancet commissions) are particularly valuable here

### Function 2: Coverage Check (after search)
- After assembling primary studies, cross-reference against existing reviews
- Did we miss important studies they included?
- Did we include studies they excluded? (Economics papers, Nepal-specific grey literature typically absent from health reviews)
- Does our reading agree with their pooled findings? If not, investigate and explain why.

### Function 3: Cited Reference (in policy briefs)
- Cite in policy briefs as supporting/divergent evidence
- Example: "A Cochrane review of 30 studies found X. Our analysis of 12 Nepal-relevant studies — including 4 economics papers not covered by the Cochrane review — is consistent but suggests the effect is moderated by..."
- This is where NHPL adds value: cross-disciplinary synthesis that traditional reviews don't do

### In the Evidence Portal (user-facing)
- Include landmark systematic reviews as evidence entries, tagged as `studyDesign: "systematic-review"` or `"meta-analysis"`
- One-pager structure differs from primary studies:
  - "What Was Studied": The review question, how many studies, what types
  - "What They Found": Pooled findings, heterogeneity, GRADE quality
  - "What This Means for Nepal": Where Nepal-specific evidence fits within the overall picture. What the review doesn't cover. What questions remain open.

## 12. NHPL's Methodological Edge

NHPL's synthesis is genuinely additive because it crosses disciplinary boundaries that traditional reviews don't:

- **Traditional systematic reviews** separate by study type. A Cochrane review of RCTs on health insurance won't include quasi-experimental economic evaluations, qualitative studies, or government programme data.
- **NHPL's policy briefs** synthesise across these silos: RCTs + economics papers + qualitative research + Nepal-specific grey literature, all contextualised for Nepal's federal system, political economy, and health system structure.
- **No existing systematic review does this.** Cochrane doesn't include economics journals. JHE doesn't include qualitative. Nobody contextualises for Nepal.

This cross-disciplinary, contextualised synthesis is the core value proposition and should be stated explicitly in all methodology documentation and funding applications.

## 13. Inclusion Criteria

A paper is **included** if it meets ALL of:

1. **Relevance**: Addresses one or more of the 10 NHPL topics
2. **Geography**: Falls within the three-tier system:
   - Tier 1: Nepal-specific (auto-include)
   - Tier 2: South Asia with transferable findings
   - Tier 3: Other LMIC with comparable context (reviewer judges transferability)
3. **Study type**: Any listed in Section 3
4. **Grey literature**: Included with AACODS appraisal
5. **Language**: English or Nepali
6. **Recency**: Prioritise post-2000. Older landmark studies included if still policy-relevant.

---

## 14. Exclusion Criteria

A paper is **excluded** if ANY of:

1. **No health policy relevance** — pure clinical/biomedical (e.g., drug mechanism lab study) with no policy, systems, or population health implications
2. **High-income country only** — unless finding is demonstrably universal
3. **Editorials, commentaries, letters** — unless containing original data or novel policy argument not available elsewhere
4. **Duplicate/superseded** — newer version exists (e.g., updated systematic review replaces older)
5. **Retracted papers**
6. **Unable to access** — can't appraise what you can't read

**Excluded edge cases:**
- Conference abstracts (flag for follow-up when full paper publishes)
- Dissertations/theses
- Blog posts / think tank opinion pieces
- Newspaper/media reports

## 16. Phasing

| Phase | Papers | Approach |
|-------|--------|----------|
| 1a (launch) | 30 verified papers | Expert-led purposive curation. Team identifies key papers from PhD reading lists, supervisor syllabi, expert recommendations. Concentrated in 3-5 topics to meet 10-paper activation threshold. |
| 1b (expansion) | 30-100 | Targeted systematic searches on 3 priority topics (Health Financing, Primary Care, Maternal & Newborn Health). Formal search strings for PubMed + EconLit. Other topics populated via expert curation. |
| 2 (depth) | 100-200 | Broader systematic searches, additional topics activated as they reach 10-paper threshold. Full search infrastructure (Rayyan, Zotero, documented PRISMA-S protocols). |
| 3 (comprehensive) | 200+ | All topics active, regular alerts, annual topic review against GBD data. |

**Phase 1a is explicitly expert-led purposive curation, not systematic search.** Both are legitimate methodologies — but they are different things and should not be conflated.

## 17. Correction, Retraction & Status Monitoring

### Automated Status Monitoring Agent
- Runs weekly
- Checks every DOI in evidence.json against CrossRef API (retraction metadata) and Retraction Watch database
- Checks if pre-prints have been published (status upgrade)
- Checks if papers have errata or corrections
- Auto-actions:
  - **Paper retracted**: Immediately marks entry as `withdrawn` with reason. Removed from active portal display. Withdrawal banner shown if page is accessed.
  - **Pre-print → published**: Auto-updates DOI, journal, citation info
  - **Erratum published**: Flags for human review of whether NHPL summary needs updating

### User Error Reporting
- **"Report an Error" button** on every paper page
- Simple form: paper slug, description of error, reporter email (optional)
- Routed to review queue, reviewed within 48 hours

### Correction Categories
| Category | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Minor (typo, formatting) | Corrected silently |
| Material (misrepresented finding, wrong contextualisation) | Corrected with visible note: "Correction [date]: [what changed]" |
| Withdrawal (source retracted, fundamental error) | Page remains with prominent "Withdrawn" banner and explanation |

### Schema Support
- `correctionHistory`: array of {date, field, oldValue, newValue, correctedBy}
- `lastStatusCheck`: date of last automated check
- `sourceStatus`: "active" | "erratum" | "retracted" | "superseded"
- `withdrawnDate`, `withdrawnReason`: for withdrawn papers
