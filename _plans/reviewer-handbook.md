## Welcome

Thank you for joining the Nepal Health Policy Lab as an evidence reviewer. You are part of a small team making rigorous global health evidence accessible and actionable for Nepal's policymakers and practitioners.

This handbook covers everything you need to know to review papers for NHPL's evidence portal. It takes about 15 minutes to read. Keep it open as a reference during your first few reviews. If anything is unclear, ask — we would rather answer questions upfront than fix errors later.

---

## 1. What NHPL Does

NHPL maintains a publicly accessible evidence portal where policymakers can browse global health research curated and contextualised for Nepal. Each paper gets a structured one-page summary:

- **What Was Studied**: The research question, setting, population, and design
- **What They Found**: Key findings in plain language
- **What This Means for Nepal**: Policy implications contextualised for Nepal's health system, demographics, and political economy

Papers enter the portal through an AI-assisted pipeline that extracts and structures the information. **Your role is to verify that the AI got it right** — and to add the human judgment that AI cannot provide, especially the Nepal contextualisation.

## 2. Your Role

### What You Do

- Review AI-generated paper summaries for accuracy
- Verify that the findings are correctly represented
- Assess whether the Nepal contextualisation is plausible and fair
- For Tier 3 papers: conduct full methodological appraisal
- Assign evidence strength and Nepal relevance ratings

- **Verifiable experience**: Evidence appraisal and critical analysis skills applicable to PhD applications, job applications, and future research roles

### Time Commitment

## 3. The Three Tiers

Papers are classified into tiers based on their source. The tier determines how much work you do.

### Tier 1: High-Prestige Journals

**Examples**: AER, Econometrica, JPE, QJE, REStud, JHE, Health Economics, JDE, AEJ:Applied, Lancet, BMJ, NEJM, Lancet Global Health, BMJ Global Health, Health Policy and Planning, Social Science & Medicine, Cochrane Reviews

**Your job**: Full review (~15-20 minutes)
- These papers have been through rigorous peer review
- Light methodology check — the referees already did deep review, but verify the AI hasn't misrepresented the methods
- Focus on: Is the AI summary accurate? Is the Nepal contextualisation defensible?
- Single reviewer required. Nothing is published without human verification.

### Tier 2: Good Journals and Established Working Papers

**Examples**: IJHPM, Bulletin of WHO, PLOS One, BMC series, Frontiers, NBER Working Papers (by established authors), World Bank Policy Research Working Papers

**Your job**: Full review with methods scan (~15-20 minutes)
- Confirm the AI summary is accurate
- Briefly assess: Is the methodology appropriate? Any obvious red flags?
- Focus on: Nepal contextualisation
- Single reviewer required

### Tier 3: Nepal-Specific, Grey Literature, Pre-Prints

**Examples**: NHRC Journal, JNMA, Nepal-specific studies in smaller journals, MoHP/DoHS reports, donor evaluations, all pre-prints (medRxiv, SSRN drafts), lesser-known working paper series

**Your job**: Full appraisal (~90-150 minutes including reading the paper)
- Deep methodological review using the appropriate checklist
- Verify every element of the AI summary
- Write or substantially revise the Nepal contextualisation
- **Dual review required** — you and a second reviewer assess independently
- Papers only appear on the portal after human verification

## 4. The Review Process

### Step-by-Step: Tier 1/2 Review

1. You receive an email with a link to the Google Form and the AI-generated one-pager
2. Read the one-pager (you do NOT need to read the full paper for Tier 1 — do so only if something seems off)
3. For Tier 2: skim the paper's methods section
4. Complete the Google Form:
   - Is the bibliographic info correct?
   - Is the study design classification right?
   - Does "What Was Studied" accurately represent the paper?
   - Does "What They Found" accurately represent the paper?
   - Is "What This Means for Nepal" plausible and defensible?
   - Are the evidence strength and Nepal relevance ratings appropriate?
5. Submit: Approve as verified, or Flag for revision with comments

### Step-by-Step: Tier 3 Review

1. You receive an email with the paper (PDF or link), the AI-generated draft one-pager, and a link to the Google Form
2. **Read the paper** — at minimum the abstract, methods, results, and discussion
3. Complete the methodological appraisal checklist (study-type-specific — see Section 5)
4. Review the AI-generated one-pager:
   - Is "What Was Studied" accurate and complete?
   - Is "What They Found" a fair representation? Any findings omitted or overstated?
   - Is "What This Means for Nepal" defensible? Would you be comfortable if a Joint Secretary at MoHP read this?
5. Suggest revisions to any section that needs correction
6. Assign your evidence strength rating (High / Moderate / Low) with justification
7. Assign your Nepal relevance score (1-5) with justification
8. Submit your decision: Verified / Needs Revision / Rejected

### What Happens After You Submit

- **Tier 1/2**: Your review goes to the NHPL team. If approved, the paper is marked "verified" on the portal. If flagged, we revise and may ask you to re-check.
- **Tier 3**: Your review is compared with the second reviewer's. If you agree, the paper is verified (or rejected). If you disagree, a senior reviewer (currently Sabin) makes the final call. You may be asked to discuss your assessment.

## 5. Methodological Appraisal Checklists

Use the checklist that matches the paper's study design. If a paper uses multiple methods, use the checklist for the primary analysis.

### 5A. Systematic Reviews / Meta-Analyses

| # | Question | Your Assessment |
|---|----------|----------------|
| 1 | Was the search comprehensive? (Multiple databases, grey literature?) | |
| 2 | Were inclusion/exclusion criteria clear and appropriate? | |
| 3 | Was risk of bias assessed for included studies? | |
| 4 | Was heterogeneity explored and explained? | |
| 5 | Were results robust to sensitivity analyses? | |
| 6 | Was GRADE or equivalent applied to rate evidence quality? | |
| 7 | Are any important studies missing that you know of? | |

**Overall methods assessment**: Adequate / Partially adequate / Inadequate

### 5B. Randomised Controlled Trials

| # | Question | Your Assessment |
|---|----------|----------------|
| 1 | Was randomisation adequate? (Sequence generation, allocation concealment) | |
| 2 | Was blinding appropriate for the intervention type? | |
| 3 | What was the attrition rate? Was it differential between groups? | |
| 4 | Was intention-to-treat analysis used? | |
| 5 | Was the sample size adequate? (Power analysis reported?) | |
| 6 | How different is the study setting from Nepal? | |
| 7 | Was the intervention delivered as designed? (Implementation fidelity) | |

**Overall methods assessment**: Adequate / Partially adequate / Inadequate

### 5C. Economics Papers (Causal Inference)

Use the **CAFE-HE framework** (separate document). CAFE-HE provides detailed appraisal guidance for:

- **Instrumental Variables (IV)**: Instrument relevance, exclusion restriction, complier characterisation, LATE policy relevance
- **Difference-in-Differences (DiD)**: Parallel trends, staggered timing, anticipation effects, composition stability
- **Regression Discontinuity (RDD)**: Manipulation testing, bandwidth sensitivity, polynomial sensitivity, local estimate relevance
- **Matching / Propensity Score**: Selection on observables, common support, balance, hidden bias sensitivity
- **Structural Models**: Assumptions, identification, model fit, counterfactual sensitivity
- **Cost-Effectiveness**: Perspective, cost transferability, threshold appropriateness, budget impact

Complete the relevant CAFE-HE subsection and the Data Quality, Results, and External Validity domains.

### 5D. Observational Studies (Non-Econometric)

| # | Question | Your Assessment |
|---|----------|----------------|
| 1 | Is the sampling strategy described? Is the sample representative? | |
| 2 | Are key confounders identified and adjusted for? | |
| 3 | Are measurement instruments validated? | |
| 4 | For cohort studies: What is the follow-up completeness? Attrition bias? | |
| 5 | For cross-sectional: Are causal claims avoided or appropriately caveated? | |
| 6 | For case-control: Is the case definition clear? Control selection appropriate? Recall bias addressed? | |
| 7 | Sample size: adequate for the claimed conclusions? | |

**Overall methods assessment**: Adequate / Partially adequate / Inadequate

### 5E. Qualitative Research

| # | Question | Your Assessment |
|---|----------|----------------|
| 1 | Is the research question appropriate for qualitative methods? | |
| 2 | Is the sampling strategy clear? (Purposive, theoretical, snowball?) | |
| 3 | Is data saturation discussed? | |
| 4 | Is researcher positionality acknowledged? (Reflexivity) | |
| 5 | Is the analytical approach clear and rigorous? (Thematic analysis, grounded theory, framework analysis?) | |
| 6 | Are findings grounded in data? (Quotes, examples provided?) | |
| 7 | Is transferability to Nepal's cultural context plausible? | |

**Overall methods assessment**: Adequate / Partially adequate / Inadequate

### 5F. Grey Literature (AACODS Framework)

| # | Criterion | Question | Your Assessment |
|---|-----------|----------|----------------|
| 1 | **Authority** | Who produced it? Is the institution credible in this domain? Are the authors qualified? | |
| 2 | **Accuracy** | Is the methodology transparent? Are data sources cited? Can findings be verified? | |
| 3 | **Coverage** | Is the scope clear? Are limitations acknowledged? | |
| 4 | **Objectivity** | Is there institutional bias? (e.g., a funder evaluating its own programme) Are conflicts of interest declared? | |
| 5 | **Date** | Is it current enough to be relevant? Have conditions changed since publication? | |
| 6 | **Significance** | Does it provide unique information not available in peer-reviewed sources? Is it relevant to the topic? | |

**Overall assessment**: Include / Include with caveats / Exclude

## 6. Nepal Contextualisation Guide

The "What This Means for Nepal" section is where NHPL adds its unique value. This is not just a summary of the paper — it is your expert judgment about how the findings apply (or don't apply) to Nepal.

### Questions to Consider

Not every question applies to every paper. Address the ones most relevant.

| # | Question |
|---|----------|
| 1 | **Health system match**: Does Nepal's structure (federal, 3 tiers, 753 palikas, public-private mix) resemble the study setting? What's critically different? |
| 2 | **Financing match**: Is Nepal's financing context (NHIP with 54% OOP, tax-funded BHSP) comparable to the study? |
| 3 | **Demographics**: Population density, rural/urban split, literacy, age structure, ethnic diversity — how similar? |
| 4 | **Economic level**: GDP per capita (~$1,400), poverty rates, inequality — comparable to the study country? |
| 5 | **Implementation feasibility**: Does Nepal have the workforce (0.17 doctors/1,000), infrastructure, and institutional capacity to do what the study tested? |
| 6 | **Cultural context**: Would caste/ethnicity dynamics, gender norms, health-seeking behaviour, or traditional medicine practices affect how the intervention works? |
| 7 | **Political economy**: How do governance capacity, donor dependence, the federalism transition, and local government capacity affect applicability? |
| 8 | **Prior Nepal experience**: Has anything similar been tried in Nepal? What happened? What can we learn from that? |

### Writing Style

- Write for a **Joint Secretary at MoHP** or a **WHO country office staffer** — someone who is educated, busy, and wants to know what to do with this evidence
- Be **specific**: "This could inform Nepal's NHIP premium structure" is better than "This is relevant to Nepal's health system"
- Be **honest about limitations**: "This finding may not transfer because..." is more valuable than false confidence
- Be **actionable**: Where possible, connect findings to specific Nepali policies, programmes, or debates
- Use **plain language**: Avoid unnecessary jargon. If you must use technical terms, explain them briefly.
- **Length**: 150-300 words. Enough to be substantive, short enough to be read.

### Common Pitfalls

- **Over-claiming transferability**: A study from Thailand (upper-middle income, unitary government, strong civil service) may not transfer well to Nepal
- **Ignoring implementation**: An intervention that works in a well-funded RCT with dedicated staff may not work in a rural health post with one ANM
- **Generic contextualisation**: "This is relevant to Nepal because Nepal is also a developing country" adds no value. Be specific about WHY it's relevant and WHAT specifically applies.
- **Ignoring politics**: The best evidence-based policy is useless if it's politically impossible. Note political feasibility where relevant.

## 7. Rating Scales

### Methodological Rigour

| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| **High** | Well-designed study with credible methodology, adequate sample size, findings robust to checks. Identification strategy (if applicable) is credible. |
| **Moderate** | Reasonable methodology with some limitations (potential confounding, small sample, limited robustness checks), but findings are informative for policy. |
| **Low** | Significant methodological limitations. Included because the topic is underserved — but limitations must be prominently flagged. |

### Nepal Applicability

| Rating | Criteria |
|--------|----------|
| **High** | Study setting closely matches Nepal's context (health system structure, income level, demographics). Findings are directly applicable with minimal contextualisation caveats. |
| **Moderate** | Some contextual similarities but important differences exist. Findings are informative but require careful contextualisation and caveats. |
| **Low** | Setting is substantially different from Nepal. Findings may be conceptually relevant but transfer requires heavy caveats. Included for the mechanism or conceptual insight, not direct applicability. |

### Nepal Relevance Score (1-5)

| Score | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **5** | Nepal-specific study, directly applicable to current policy question | A study of NHIP enrollment in Bagmati Province |
| **4** | Nepal-specific or South Asian study, highly relevant | A Bangladesh study on community health insurance with similar population |
| **3** | Comparable LMIC, transferable with some contextualisation | A Rwandan study on performance-based financing — different continent but similar income level and health system challenges |
| **2** | Relevant topic but context differs significantly | A Brazilian study on family health teams — relevant concept but middle-income country with very different system |
| **1** | Relevant topic, but setting requires heavy contextualisation | A US or European study with conceptual relevance but limited direct applicability |

## 8. Disagreement Resolution (Tier 3 Dual Review)

| Scenario | What Happens |
|----------|-------------|
| Both reviewers: "Verified," scores within 1 point | Verified. Average the scores. |
| Both reviewers: "Verified," scores differ by 2+ points | Reviewers discuss and reconcile. If no agreement, senior reviewer decides. |
| One "Verified," one "Rejected" | Senior reviewer reads both assessments and the paper, makes final call. |
| Both "Rejected" | Rejected. Reasons documented. |
| Disagreement on factual accuracy | Summary revised, may be re-reviewed. |
| Disagreement on contextualisation | Senior reviewer makes final call. |

You will never be penalised for disagreeing with another reviewer. Honest disagreement improves quality.

## 9. Conflict of Interest Policy

You **cannot** review a paper if:

- You are an author or co-author
- Your PhD supervisor is an author
- A close collaborator is an author
- You have a financial interest in the findings

If you're unsure, disclose and we'll decide together. When in doubt, recuse yourself.

## 10. What "Verified" Means

When you mark a paper as "Verified," you are confirming:

1. ✅ The bibliographic information is correct
2. ✅ The study design classification is accurate
3. ✅ The methodological rigour rating is appropriate
3b. ✅ The Nepal applicability rating is appropriate
4. ✅ The one-pager summary accurately represents the paper's findings
5. ✅ The Nepal contextualisation is plausible and fair
6. ✅ Material limitations are noted
7. ✅ Policy implications are defensible

For Tier 3 papers, additionally:

8. ✅ Methodology is sound (or limitations clearly flagged)
9. ✅ Sample size / statistical power is adequate for claimed conclusions
10. ✅ Identification strategy is credible (for econometric papers)
11. ✅ Findings are not overstated relative to the evidence

This is what you are putting your name to. If you are uncomfortable signing off on any point, flag it for revision or rejection.
